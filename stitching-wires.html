The range

Seven grades of stitching wire.

Prime Wires draws stitching wire for corrugated boxes, books, cheque books, leaflets and printed work — seven grades, each drawn for the box or the spine it has to close. Sections run 20 to 26 gauge flat, on spools from 2 kg for hand and bindery work up to 25 kg for the automatics.

Coating weight and tensile band are stated for every grade. The section tolerance holds along the full length of a 25 kg reel because we draw our own rod rather than re-spooling wire bought in finished — which also means that when a feed problem comes back to us, we can trace it to the cast, the draw and the bath for that reel number and fix it at the operation that caused it.

01 · The volume grade

G.I. stitching wire

Mild steel core, electroplated with zinc, rolled flat. It is the wire most Indian corrugated plants run and the baseline every other grade is priced against. For a carton that ships dry and is opened within a few weeks it is the correct engineering answer, not merely the cheap one.

02 · Heavier zinc

Rust resistant stitching wire

The trade calls it R.P. wire. Identical steel core to G.I., with a materially heavier zinc deposit that pushes back the onset of surface rust — for monsoon-season runs, uncontrolled inland storage and cartons that sit for a season before they ship.

04 · Solid stainless, magnetic or non-magnetic

Stainless steel stitching wire

Solid stainless steel with no coating to wear through, in two families that behave very differently on a packing line. Ferritic grades are magnetic and register on an in-line metal detector. Austenitic grades are effectively non-magnetic and are specified where a magnetic response is itself the problem.

06 · Solid alloy, rust-free

Pure brass stitching wire

Drawn from solid copper-zinc alloy with no mild steel anywhere in the section. There is no coating to wear through and no substrate underneath to corrode — cut it, bend it, abrade it, clinch it, and every surface you expose is the same alloy you started with.

07 · Solid copper, softest clinch

Pure copper stitching wire

Solid drawn electrolytic copper. Like brass it cannot rust — there is no iron in the section to form the red oxide that stains board and weakens the fibre around a stitch. It will darken to a patina over time, which is surface oxide and cosmetic, and it does not travel into the carton.

Format · not a grade

Book & narrow flat stitching wire

Saddle stitching, side stitching and spiral work, in 24 to 26 gauge on 2 and 5 kg spools. Available in galvanised, copper coated, brass and pure copper.

A narrow flat section gives a broader crown bearing on the spine than round wire of the same mass, which means less cut-through on thin cover stock and a binding that survives handling for longer. Many binders outside India moved to flat for exactly this reason. It needs a head rated for flat wire — check before ordering.
