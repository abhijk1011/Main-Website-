The range

Steel wire, drawn at Waliv.

Everything in this range is drawn on our own benches at Waliv, from rod intake to finished coil. Mild steel wire in hard, half-hard and annealed tempers; high carbon wire patented and drawn to tensile for springs and rope; and zinc-coated wire for outdoor and industrial service. Chemistry, temper and coating class are stated for every product, so compare them against whatever you are running now.

Sub-group 1 of 2

Mild steel, drawn.

H.B. wire, H.H.B. wire, annealed wire, binding wire, baling wire. One steel, five tempers and finishes, each drawn for what happens to the wire next.

01 · Hard drawn, bright finish

H.B. wire (hard bright)

Low carbon mild steel rod, descaled and drawn cold through successive dies with no anneal after the final pass. The wire keeps every bit of the work hardening the draw puts into it, which is where its stiffness and its tensile come from, and pickling before drawing leaves the surface bright rather than scaled.

02 · Half hard, formable

H.H.B. wire (half hard bright)

The same low carbon steel as H.B., process annealed back to a half-hard temper after drawing. Tensile comes down and ductility goes up, and that is the entire point of the grade. A cold-headed nail, screw or rivet is formed by upsetting the wire against a die, and wire that is too hard splits at the head instead of flowing into it.

03 · Soft, fully annealed

Annealed wire

Drawn mild steel wire taken through a controlled anneal that relieves the internal stress the draw put into it. What comes off the other end bends, twists and folds without cracking and, more to the point for a manufacturer, feeds through high-speed downstream machinery without the breakages that stop a line and take twenty minutes to re-thread.

04 · Soft annealed, for tying

Binding wire

Soft annealed mild steel wire for rebar tying and general bundling. Gauge is held tight for a practical reason at both ends of the tolerance: wire that runs thin snaps in the twist and costs a man on site his rhythm, and wire that runs thick wastes metal on every single tie across a slab.

05 · Heavy duty, high tension

Baling wire

Wire for baling presses: scrap, waste paper, textile and agricultural. A bale tie carries its load in tension for months and takes a shock every time the bale is lifted, dropped or shunted, so what actually matters is not tensile alone but the elongation left in the wire alongside it. A tie with no ductility left in it fails at the first knock.

06 · Patented and cold drawn

High carbon wire

Unalloyed high carbon steel, patented and cold drawn to tensile. This is a different steel from everything above it on this page. The carbon runs two to four times that of mild steel, and the wire is worked to hold a load and return to shape rather than to bend and stay bent.

07 · Zinc coated

G.I. wire

Mild steel wire carrying a zinc coating. Zinc is anodic to steel, so it corrodes first and goes on protecting the steel underneath even where the coating has been scratched through, which is the whole reason galvanised wire survives outdoors and bright wire does not.

Reference

Standards referenced in this category.

IS 280 : 2006
Mild steel wire for general engineering purposes. Covers chemistry, diameter tolerance, tensile and the wrapping test for H.B., H.H.B., annealed, binding and baling wire, and for the base wire under G.I.
IS 4826 : 1979
Hot-dipped galvanized coatings on round steel wires. Sets minimum coating mass by diameter for light, medium and heavy classes, plus adhesion and uniformity testing.
IS 4454 (Part 1) : 2001
Steel wire for mechanical springs, patented and cold drawn, unalloyed. Sets the SL / SM / SH and DM / DH grades and their tensile bands by diameter.
