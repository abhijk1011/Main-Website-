How the number is worked out

No assumptions hidden in the arithmetic.

Cross-sectional area is thickness multiplied by width. Mass per millimetre of wire is that area multiplied by the density of the material — 7.85 g/cm³ for mild steel, 8.50 for brass, 8.90 for copper. Multiply by the length consumed in one stitch to get the mass of a single pin, then by the price per kilogram.

That is the whole calculation. It ignores nothing that matters and includes nothing that does not: no scrap allowance, no wastage factor, no margin. If you want those in, add them to the price per kilogram.

Two things to sanity-check. First, wire consumed per stitch is the input people get wrong — measure a fired stitch off your own head and straighten it rather than guessing. Second, the index prices pre-filled here move with the metal market, particularly for copper and brass. Overwrite them with your live quote before you take this to a costing meeting.
