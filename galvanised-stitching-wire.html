Stitching Wire 01 · The volume grade

G.I. stitching wire.

Mild steel core, electroplated with zinc, rolled flat. It is the wire most Indian corrugated plants run and the baseline every other grade is priced against. For a carton that ships dry and is opened within a few weeks it is the correct engineering answer, not merely the cheap one.

Where it belongs

What it is drawn for.

The uniform section and clean surface are what keep a high-speed head fed without stopping, which is where the real cost sits on a volume line.

Applications
FMCG cartons Dry storage Local distribution

Fast-rotating FMCG cartons, dry covered warehousing, local distribution, and any box opened within weeks of being closed. Specify something heavier for sea export, coastal or monsoon storage, or pharmaceutical and food cartons where a rust mark means rejection.

Compatibility

Runs on every common head.

Galvanised is the most tolerant grade in the range. If a stitching machine runs flat wire at all, it will run this.

Technical FAQ

Galvanised stitching wire, answered.

What is galvanised stitching wire?

Galvanised stitching wire, commonly called G.I. stitching wire, is a low carbon mild steel wire that has been electroplated with a layer of zinc and then rolled to a flat section for use in box stitching machines. The zinc layer gives the wire its bright silver finish and provides a first line of defence against surface rust. Typical coating weight is 8 to 12 grams per square metre.

Is galvanised stitching wire rust proof?

No. Galvanised stitching wire is rust resistant, not rust proof. The zinc is a coating over a mild steel core, so where the coating is thin, scratched or worn through by the stitching head, the steel underneath can corrode. For genuinely rust-free stitching you need a solid alloy with no steel in it, which means pure brass or pure copper stitching wire.

What gauge does galvanised stitching wire come in?

Prime Wires galvanised stitching wire is supplied in flat sections from 20 gauge, nominally 0.90 by 2.20 mm, down to 26 gauge, nominally 0.45 by 1.15 mm. Most single and double wall corrugated carton work runs on 23 or 24 gauge. Heavier multi-wall board takes 20 to 22 gauge. Book and saddle stitching takes 25 to 26 gauge.

How much does one stitch of galvanised wire cost?

At 23 gauge, a typical corrugated stitch consuming about 45 millimetres of wire uses roughly 0.36 grams of wire. At an indicative rate of 92 rupees per kilogram, that is approximately 3.3 paise per stitch, or around 26 paise for an eight-stitch carton. The cost per pin calculator on this site works it out for your own section, price and stitch count.

What is the difference between galvanised and rust resistant stitching wire?

The base metal is identical low carbon mild steel. The difference is the amount of zinc deposited during electroplating. Standard galvanised carries 8 to 12 grams per square metre. Rust resistant, known in the trade as R.P. wire, carries 18 to 25. Power G.I. stitching wire carries 30 to 40. Heavier zinc costs more per kilogram and delays the onset of surface rust proportionally.
